1943 – 1945

The Story of the 95th

Three Distinguished Unit Citations. 334 missions. The first daylight raid on Berlin. The highest number of enemy aircraft destroyed by any USAAF Bomb Group in World War II.

Timeline

Key Milestones

1942
Formation

The 95th Bomb Group is activated in the United States and begins training on the Boeing B-17 Flying Fortress. Crews are drawn from across America, many barely out of school.

May 1943
First Mission from Horham

The 95th flies its first combat mission on 13 May 1943. Based at AAF Station 119, Horham, Suffolk, the group quickly establishes itself as one of the foremost in the Eighth Air Force.

Jun 1943
First Distinguished Unit Citation

The group receives its first Distinguished Unit Citation for extraordinary heroism and determination during a mission to Kiel, Germany — the beginning of a remarkable record of citation.

Aug 1943
Regensburg Mission

The 95th participates in the famous Regensburg–Schweinfurt double-strike mission — one of the most costly and courageous operations of the entire air war. The group fights through fierce opposition to reach its target.

Mar 1944
First to Berlin — The Historic Raid

On 4 March 1944, the 95th Bomb Group becomes the first American bomb group to perform a daylight raid on Berlin — a milestone that carries profound symbolic and strategic significance in the war against Nazi Germany.

1944–45
Third Distinguished Unit Citation

The 95th goes on to earn two more Distinguished Unit Citations, the only Eighth Air Force Bomb Group ever to receive three. Throughout the campaign, the group claimed 425 enemy aircraft destroyed in air combat — the highest total of any USAAF Bomb Group in the war.

May 1945
Final Mission

The 95th Bomb Group flies its final mission on 25 May 1945, just days after the German surrender. The group returns to the United States, leaving behind the fields of Suffolk and the memory of those who did not come home.

Today
The Red Feather Club Keeps the Flame

The 95th Bomb Group Heritage Association preserves and interprets this extraordinary history from Horham — in the very buildings where the men of the 95th once gathered, danced, drank, and prepared to fly.
